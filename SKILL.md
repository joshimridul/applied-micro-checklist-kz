# Applied microeconomics writing

Apply Ekaterina Zhuravskaya's eight-page memo, *Rules for Good Academic Writing in Applied Economics: A Checklist of Dos and Don'ts*, to the writing task the user requested. The memo concerns the **form of applied-microeconomics papers, not their research content**.

## Use the source faithfully

Read the relevant sections of the [source-derived checklist](references/memo-checklist.md) before drafting or editing. For a whole-paper audit, read all twelve sections. The [original memo](references/original-memo.pdf) is the authority if wording or attribution is uncertain.

Apply the memo's rules at their stated strength. Do not soften an imperative, turn a recommendation into a requirement, or add advice from another writing guide. Preserve these distinctions in particular:

- Putting exhibits at the end and omitting citations from conclusions are explicitly the author's preferences.
- The 5–6-page introduction benchmark applies to an applied paper without a model or structural estimation and assumes standard formatting.
- The 100–150-word abstract target, table-precision guidance, and footnote limits retain the wording and qualifications given in the checklist.
- American-English conventions do not erase the memo's stated British-English qualifications.

If an explicit user instruction or supplied venue requirement conflicts with the memo, retain the instruction and identify the departure. Do not claim that the source rule is different. If the user asks for strict memo compliance, report the conflict rather than silently resolving it.

## Stay within the requested scope

Determine whether the user asked to draft, revise, copyedit, or audit. Work only at that level.

- **Draft:** Use the supplied research question, evidence, and terminology. Return the requested prose.
- **Revise or copyedit:** Change only the requested material. Preserve the paper's factual meaning, estimates, uncertainty, samples, citations, and LaTeX structure. Follow the requested clean-text, marked-edit, or diff format.
- **Audit:** Audit only when requested. For a focused audit, apply the relevant memo topics. For a whole-paper audit, cover all twelve topics and use [the review template](templates/review.md) when useful. Give the location, rule ID, reader-facing problem, and practical repair for each consequential finding.

Do not append an unsolicited audit to a drafting or local-editing request. Do not introduce econometric specifications, identification assumptions, robustness exercises, citations, results, or policy conclusions that the user did not supply. When a research-content question prevents a writing judgment, identify it as outside this memo's scope instead of solving it.

## Verify only what the material supports

Do not invent missing table means, model descriptions, references, sample definitions, or figure details. Rules about page length, paragraph height, footnote density, final exhibit type size, and figure sharpness require rendered pages. Rules about numbering require the relevant references and targets. Mark an item as not checked when the necessary material was not supplied or inspected.

Reviewing a supplied bibliography does not establish that all relevant recent work is cited. Do not perform or imply an external literature search unless the user requested one.

## Return the requested result

Lead with the requested artifact or audit. Keep commentary proportional to the task. In an audit, distinguish observed problems, satisfied rules, deliberate departures, inapplicable rules, and checks that could not be performed. State what material and rendered pages were inspected.

Do not offer methodological recommendations or a judgment about research validity under this skill. Apply the memo's writing guidance—no less and no more.
